Android App Development Cost: What Drives It from First Version to Enterprise Scale

App development quotes can differ several times over for ideas that sound the same. These are the factors that really drive cost, how to start with a first version, and what to prepare before asking for a quote.

Raphael Wicaksono · October 30, 2026 · 2 min read

Contents
  1. What drives the cost
  2. Start with a first version people use
  3. Costs after launch
  4. Prepare this before asking for a quote
  5. How to compare vendors

"How much does an Android app cost?" is like "how much does a house cost?". It depends on how many rooms, for whom, and what is connected to it. Two attendance apps can differ widely if one only records clock-in time while the other uses face recognition, GPS location, shifts, and calculates payroll directly.

What drives the cost

  • Number of user roles: employees, supervisors, HR, and admins each have different screens and permissions.
  • Number and complexity of features: a simple form is far lighter than multi-level approvals or automatic calculations.
  • Integrations: connecting to HRIS, ERP, payroll, CCTV, or legacy databases is often the most time-consuming part.
  • Offline mode: on-device storage and syncing when signal returns need dedicated design.
  • Hardware: camera, GPS, NFC, Bluetooth, or printers add testing.
  • Web dashboard: almost every business app needs an admin side for summaries, approvals, and reports.
  • Platforms: Android only, or Android and iOS together.
  • Security and personal data: login, encryption, activity logs, and data retention rules.

Start with a first version people use

The safest approach is to build a first version with the core features, use it with one team or one site, then expand. For a field app:

  1. First version: login, one or two main forms, photos and location, and a summary in the dashboard.
  2. Next stage: supervisor approvals, notifications, and offline mode.
  3. Enterprise scale: HRIS or ERP integration, multiple branches, advanced reports, and finer user roles.

This keeps the first budget smaller, and later features are chosen from real usage rather than guesses.

Costs after launch

An app is not a one-off purchase. Also include:

  • Server or hosting for the backend and database, in the cloud or on your own server.
  • Developer accounts if the app is published on the Play Store or App Store.
  • Updates for new Android versions and new devices.
  • Fixes and user support, including when rules or work processes change.
  • Further feature development once the app is in use.

Prepare this before asking for a quote

  • The problem to solve, in one or two sentences.
  • Who the users are and how many.
  • The current workflow, such as photos of paper forms or chat group screenshots.
  • Systems that must connect and who manages them.
  • Field conditions: signal, phone types in use, and whether devices are shared.
  • Target timeline and when the first version must be in use.

How to compare vendors

  • Does the quote break features down by stage, not just one total?
  • Are the web dashboard and backend included?
  • Who holds the code, the server, and the Play Store account?
  • How are field testing and user training done?
  • What is included in post-launch support?
